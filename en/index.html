Maybe you’ll recognise yourself

Almost nobody says it out loud

  • An ordinary Monday, in the car with the engine off, outside the building of the job they chased for half a lifetime. Nothing stops them going up. And they don’t.
  • An October night, on the sofa, after getting everyone’s next day sorted. They switch on the TV without looking at what’s on, and a question arrives: and where am I in all this?
  • Six years from retirement, mortgage paid off, staring at the ceiling in the small hours. Doing the sums, and they aren’t about money.

None of them is carrying a tragedy, and maybe that’s why none of them asks for help. What they have is a silent crack between the life they lead and the person leading it.

If you saw yourself in any of the three, what you feel is legitimate, even if from the outside it looks like you lack nothing. It has an explanation, and there is a way out.

I’ve been there too

A morning in Querétaro

About ten years ago I was living in Mexico with my family, posted abroad for work. One morning, just out of bed, I was looking out of the window at the low houses of Querétaro pressed against the new buildings, and I felt an unease I couldn’t place. Not sadness, not anxiety, just a background discomfort, like when something doesn’t fit and you don’t yet know what it is.

For years I had been working out how to meet my obligations better. That morning I understood that the real question was why I had turned my whole life into an obligation. I also know what it is to spend two years knowing a job is no longer yours, with the diagnosis crystal clear in your head and your life unchanged.

I’m not a psychologist or a coach. I speak to you from the other side of the table, as someone who achieved almost everything he set out to do and felt the numbers didn’t add up. IKIGAIER was born from that search.
