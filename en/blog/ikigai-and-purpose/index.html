Is ikigai the same as life purpose?

They are used as synonyms, but they aren’t quite. And the difference isn’t academic: it changes what you look for and how frustrated you get looking for it.

Manel Rueda Author and creator of IKIGAIER 21 September 2026

Since the famous ikigai diagram was actually born talking about purpose, it makes sense that many people use the two words as if they were the same. “Ikigai” would simply sound like the pretty Japanese version of “life purpose”. They are alike, yes. But treating them as identical leads you to look for the wrong thing.

What we mean by purpose

Purpose, as the word is used in the West, tends to be a grand direction: a mission, a goal that organises the rest of your life. It’s usually singular (“finding my purpose”), looks to the future and comes with a certain epic quality: something important to do, a reason you came into the world.

That idea has its strength, but also a trap: if you believe there is one purpose and you have to find it, it’s very easy to feel you’re missing it, that it hasn’t arrived yet, that others have it and you don’t.

What ikigai is, by contrast

Ikigai, in its Japanese sense, is more everyday and more plural. It isn’t the reason for your existence, but the reasons —often small and many— why it’s worth getting up today. You can have many at once, they live mostly in the present and they don’t need to be big or profitable.

The difference in one line

Purpose answers “what did I come here for?”. Ikigai answers “why is this day worth it?”. They are not the same question.

Why the difference matters

Because it changes the task. If you set out to find the purpose of your life, it’s easy to freeze, waiting for a revelation that puts everything in order at once. If you understand that you can recognise several reasons to live that are already present, you stop looking for something huge and start paying attention to what you already have.

Looking for “the purpose” leaves you in debt. Recognising your ikigai brings you back to your own life.

Inherited purpose and your own purpose

There’s a nuance that matters to me especially at IKIGAIER, and it isn’t Japanese tradition but my own reading: much of what we call “my purpose” isn’t ours. It’s an inherited purpose: what was expected of us, the career we were meant to have, the success we were supposed to chase. You can spend years pushing towards a goal you never actually chose.

Telling ikigai apart from purpose helps precisely there. While “purpose” can still carry the weight of expectation, ikigai makes you come down to the concrete: what makes today worthwhile for you? And that question is much harder to answer with someone else’s life.

So, which should you look for?

You don’t have to choose one over the other, but it helps to know which one you’re looking for. If “finding your purpose” has you stuck, try lowering the bar and looking at your ikigai: the small things that already hold you up. Sometimes the great purpose doesn’t appear when you look for it head-on; it takes shape on its own when you stop betraying the small things that matter to you.
