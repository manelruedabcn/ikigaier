What is ikigaier

ikigaier is the approach to ikigai that I, Manel Rueda, have created. It starts from a simple idea: ikigai has two sides, what you do and how you live it. An ikigaier is someone who learns not to separate the two.

The word is new. It isn’t Japanese, and it isn’t a typo for ikigai: I use it to name a particular way of looking at ikigai, the one I develop in my books, the test and the workshops.

The two sides of ikigai

The ikigai you find online, the one with four overlapping circles, describes what we do fairly well. What it misses is where we do it from. That’s why I distinguish two dimensions. It’s my own elaboration, not a Japanese tradition.

active ikigai

The dimension of doing: what you build, what you produce, what you give to others. It’s what the popular diagram tries to organise.

conscious ikigai

The dimension of being: the presence and coherence with which you live what you do. It isn’t measured in achievements.

You can have plenty of the first and very little of the second. It’s what happens to many people who have achieved almost everything and still feel something doesn’t fit.

Where it comes from

About ten years ago I drew four circles on a napkin to explain them to my children. Around the same years, while living in Querétaro, Mexico, I woke one morning with an unease I couldn’t place: I had achieved almost everything I had set out to do, and the sums didn’t add up.

Over the years I realised the diagram helped me organise what I did, but told me nothing about how I was living it. ikigaier grew out of that search. I tell more of my story on my About page.

How it’s practised

ikigaier is practised alone and in a group:

  • The free ikigai test: 16 questions on both sides of ikigai.
  • The free one-month programme, with exercises by email.
  • The books (in Spanish): No todo lo que te frena es tuyo, Disciplina para indisciplinados, El Ikigai que no te contaron and the workbook Camina sin separarte de ti.
  • The in-person workshops, where we practise the method’s five gestures: stopping and listening, looking at where you’ve drifted from yourself, recognising the masks, coming down from the mind into the body, and choosing a first step.

What ikigaier is not

It isn’t coaching or therapy, and I’m not a psychologist. It won’t tell you what to do with your life, nor does it promise you’ll find meaning in a weekend. It’s a way of looking, with simple tools, that I share from my own experience.

Frequently asked questions

What does ikigaier mean?

It’s the approach to ikigai that brings together active ikigai, what you do and contribute, and conscious ikigai, how you live it. An ikigaier is someone who learns not to separate the two.

Is ikigaier the same as ikigai?

No. Ikigai is a Japanese word usually translated as what makes life worth living. ikigaier is a new word I use to name my way of working with it.

Who created ikigaier?

I did: Manel Rueda. I’m an engineer, writer and facilitator, and I live near Barcelona.

Where do I start?

With the ikigai test. It’s free, needs no sign-up, and at the end it shows you what holds you up, what needs attention and an exercise for your week.
